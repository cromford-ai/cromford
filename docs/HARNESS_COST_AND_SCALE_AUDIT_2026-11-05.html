  • The factory pays twice for most of its work. $2,225 in 7 days went to a prompt fingerprint called again within one hour, $1,947 of it in the execute stage. 278 rows that ended rc≠0 still cost $1,322. 80 calls killed by the context ceiling cost $536.

  • The selection gate discards builds on reds it cannot attribute. On 2026-11-04, 27 of 38 red gates had a baseline that could not run. The same 10 host-sensitive suites were red across unrelated tickets. Filed as #9061.
  • Fly is 80% of infrastructure and half of it is idle. The 4 performance-16x test hosts cost $2,112 per month and sit under 5% CPU in 58% of 5-minute windows, with 0 idle stops since 10-29. Pool machines spend 52% of their hours outside any model session, 59% on cromford.

  • The reviews stack. Each diff gets the in-build lenses, a fix round, a re-review, then the lead review with break-it, then a conductor re-review on each head move, then review-autopilot and review-rescue. Lead review cost $498 fleet-wide, 454 calls, once per head: 117 heads for 88 PRs.
  • The fleet polls instead of listening. 184 cron lane fragments plus 7 per-project lanes make about 123,800 ticks per day. 36 lanes can call a model; 97% of blocked-resolver ticks made no call but each read 894 candidates for 13 minutes. 66% of triage session calls had no new input and still cost $53.
  • Two stages still run on Fable 5.1 at 2.5 times the Opus token price: stage.plan and stage.preplan. The route rows say Opus everywhere else. One row change each.
  • The meters have holes. Both Claude extra-usage probes answer no-login since 10-14 and 09-28, so overage is unknown. 43% of project-6-os pool machines have no cost row. 53 rows have no price. xAI spend is computed, not metered. Fireworks and Codex have no meter.
  • 1000x is a coordination problem, not a compute problem. Compute per merged PR must fall about 10x (one test run per tree, right-sized machines, dedup before spend) and the serial waits must leave (event-driven wakes, no polling, no baseline timeouts). The plan in section 8 names each lever with its measured dollars.

The review stack on one diff

In-build lenses run in parallel on the diff (general, security on risky paths, refute on schema or money paths), then one fix round, then a re-review. Then the lead review reads the same head again with break-it on medium and high tiers, up to 3 attempts per head. The conductor re-reviews on each head move, with no daily cap. review-autopilot and review-rescue re-read aging heads. The blocked-resolver buys up to 2 fix rounds plus re-reviews per ticket and up to 3 fresh rebuilds, each with all lenses again. patch-same replay skips a repeat only when the patch is byte-equal. Median review cost per merged cromford PR is $3.62 and 5 calls; p90 is $16.89 and 15 calls; the sum is $691. Lead review alone cost $498 across the fleet in 7 days, 454 calls, and is 34% of project-6-os review spend.

Polling

The hub crontab has 190 active lines. 184 lane fragments plus 7 per-project lanes on 17 projects make about 123,800 ticks per day. 36 lane kinds can call a model; they tick about 33,000 times per day. Most lanes carry a change gate (a head sha, a fingerprint, a claim row), so a tick without new input makes no call. The exceptions: orc-monitor every 5 minutes with no dedup (its script lives outside the repo), and the calendar-keyed lanes (claude-auth-check canary, archie, archie-fm-watch, archie-research, rule-miner, lookback, pm-groom). The cost is not the calls but the reads: the blocked-resolver made 122 model calls in 7 days across 4,192 ticks, yet each cromford tick read 894 to 931 candidates for 13 minutes (fixed in part by #9036 on 11-04, candidates now 755 and the tick drains). Triage session mode made 265 of 401 calls with new=0, chg=0, due=0, for $53.

7. The 1000x plan

1000x means two things at once: 1000 times more merged PRs per week at the same budget, and each PR merged in minutes instead of hours. Today the fleet merges about 370 bot PRs per week for about $12,100 in priced model spend plus about $2,300 in infrastructure and CI, with a median cromford PR at 2 rounds, 6 prequeue starts and several hours of wall time. The measured leaks say most of the cost and most of the wall time is coordination, not compute. The plan removes the coordination waste first, because that is where 10x lives, then changes the machine shape for the next 10x, and only then buys more boxes for the last 10x.

Lever 1: pay once per unit of work

  • One suite-result ledger for the whole pipeline. Key: tree hash + suite path + runner image digest. Writers: the builder gate, the selection gate, the pre-queue gate, the batch, nightly. Readers: all of them. A green result on a tree is green for every gate that asks about that tree. Expected: the 4 to 6 runs become 1 to 2. Today's reuse ledger (BH-5354) exists but CI does not trust it; the trust rule changes to "trusted when the runner image digest matches".
  • Dedup before spend at the agent-call choke point. A persisted check on the guard fingerprint before the model call, with three terminal states (ok, empty, failed), an attempt cap of 3, and a replay of the stored answer on repeat. The metered-API rule already says this; the measurement says $2,225 per week ignores it.
  • Checkpoint and resume the execute stage. A timeout or a context kill resumes from the last commit on the branch with a short brief, instead of a fresh execute. Arm the kills at the declared ceilings.
  • Semantic head-bound verdicts. A restamp commit, a clean merge from main, or a lockfile-only change keeps the verdict. The lead review runs once per ticket per real diff.

Lever 2: event-driven, not clock-driven

  • Every model lane wakes on a row, never on a tick. GitHub webhooks, the board's own events and the jobs worker already feed a wakes table; the remaining calendar lanes and orc-monitor move onto it. The hub crontab shrinks from 190 lines to the handful of true schedules (digests, retention, canaries).
  • The resolver and the conductor read deltas. A candidate list that costs 13 minutes to discover becomes a query on a state table with an index: "parks that changed since my last tick". #9029 landed the first cut; the next cut moves discovery into D1 with the retention rule.
  • The pool spawns on queue depth, not on a 540-second tick. Fly machines start in seconds; a build that waits 9 minutes for the next tick is wall time with no work.

Lever 3: right-size every machine to what it waits on

  • Two machine classes in the pool. Plan, review, lead review and triage wait on the model: shared-cpu-2x at $13 per month-equivalent. Execute and the gates need CPU: performance-8x for the minutes they run tests, then hand back. Expected: half of pool spend.
  • Test hosts scale to zero. A gate request starts a host, a 10-minute idle timer stops it. Four hosts at 30% average CPU become two hosts most of the day. Expected: $1,200 to $1,800 per month.
  • Lane machines on shared CPU. lane-run-mk and lane-run-ts run network-bound lanes at 2 to 5% CPU on performance sizes.
  • Model tiers by stage. Opus for execute and review, Sonnet for plan drafts and triage, Haiku for canaries, classifiers and mechanical rungs. Fable nowhere in the pipeline.

Lever 4: stop the work that merges nothing

  • Poison suites get a dead letter. A suite red on 3 unrelated tickets in 24 hours is quarantined with an alert and a ticket, and the gate notes it instead of discarding the build (#9061 does the unattributed half).
  • Plan questions carry a default. 16 PLAN_BLOCKED tickets cost $201 and produced nothing. The rule from 2026-11-03 (recommendation, reasons, default) applies to the planner too: a plan that has a default proceeds and records the assumption.
  • A ticket budget stops the ladder. BH-8817 bought 20 machines and $356. The resolver's ladder (2 fix rounds, 3 rebuilds, each with all lenses) is where the money goes; after the second rebuild the ticket goes to a person with the record, not to a third rebuild.

Lever 5: measure what you pay for

  • Cash first. The extra-usage probes must log in; the 6 Max accounts and their overage become rows with a price. Until then the fleet steers on API-equivalent numbers and cannot see the bill.
  • Every machine has a cost row. 43% of project-6-os pool machines have none. A machine with no cost row is a machine the dash cannot see.
  • Cost per merged PR is the SLO per project, with a daily ceiling per lane already in place (BH-5526). The number to drive to: under $10 per merged PR and under 2 build jobs per merged PR on every project, which cromford's own peers already reach on half the stack.
What the levers add up to. Levers 1 and 4 remove the duplicated and the dead work, about 55% of today's model spend. Lever 3 halves the Fly bill. Lever 2 removes most of the wall time between stages. Together they move the fleet from about $59 to under $10 per cromford PR and from hours to tens of minutes per PR, which is the first 10x. The next 10x is more projects and more boxes on the same hub, which the event-driven hub can carry because it no longer burns its own time on ticks. The last 10x is many hubs, one per account or region, which the hub-thinning plan already names.
