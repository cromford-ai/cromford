Twelve days of the harness: half the PRs per day, a fifth of the cost per day

Same rules as before: the harness and nothing else, every logged call priced from its own token counts at list API rates. Since Sep 21 the harness merged 1,043 pull requests across nine repos and closed 819 ai-build issues, at 87 PRs a day. The model work behind it comes to $21.5K: $1.8K a day and $21 per merged PR, down from $62. Tokens per PR fell from 54M to 20M after the Sep 22 move to Opus 5.5, and the OpenRouter and alert lanes that had been leaking money went quiet. Output fell too, though. 72% of the PRs went into the harness itself, and the last four days averaged 39 PRs a day. At this window's average, the harness does the work of about 170 engineers and leads, which would cost ~$128K a day in the US. On its best proven day it did the work of about 465.

What the potential means

  • The capacity is there, and most of it went unused. The fleet has merged 237 PRs in a day. This window averaged 37% of that, and the last four days 16%.
  • Running at the proven peak, with today's cost structure: about $4.9K a day of model work for what a ~465-person engineering org ships (~$350K a day, ~$88M a year in the US). The harness would cost about $1.8M a year at list API rates.
  • Peak days have cost more per PR, though: $42 on Sep 22 and $73 on Sep 18. So $4.9K to $10K a day is the honest range, still 35–71× cheaper.
  • Client product used a small slice of it. The eight client and product repos got 289 PRs, 24 a day. The rest was the harness building itself.
  • Not measured here: why output fell. Spend dropped along with merges ($300–$560 a day since Sep 30), so the fleet looks under-fed or paused rather than at a compute limit. This report did not read queue depth, pause flags or the boards to confirm that.

Why it got cheaper

Each of these is visible in the rows. Together they account for the drop. The cost per PR fell by two thirds while the average PR shrank by only a fifth (1,159 → 920 added lines).

Opus 5.5 replaced Fable

43% → 14%

Fable's share of spend. Last window Fable cost $16.9K, direct and via OpenRouter. This window it cost $3.0K. Opus 5.5 now carries the build lanes: $8.0K across 8,192 calls, about $0.98 a call.

Context per PR collapsed

54M → 20M

Tokens per merged PR, with calls per PR down from 31 to 17. Uncached input fell from 7.0B to 1.6B over a window three times as long. Most of last window's uncached input was the no-cache OpenRouter rung.

The OpenRouter rung went quiet

$6.3K → $2.0K

The survival ladder's Claude-on-OpenRouter fallback, billed at full input price. $1.6K of this window's $2.0K landed on Sep 22. After Sep 29 it was under $5 a day.

The alert lanes stopped spending

$2.9K → ~$0

Alert-triage and responder together, last window, on no PRs at all. This window they made 10 calls.

Triage got cheaper in step

$2,640 → $497 / day

Triage's share held at 28%, so it shrank at the same rate as everything else. project-4 is now the costliest triage lane by far: $2.5K, 65% of that project's spend.

Codex carried more of the load

$2.4K → $3.7K

On the ChatGPT subscription, so the money wasn't extra. It peaked Sep 27 at two thirds of the day's spend and stopped on Sep 30.

Human equivalent

The same two methods as before, applied to the 289-hour daily average. PRs per day fell 45%, lines per day 56%, because the average PR got 20% smaller.

Method A · by merged PRs

90–105 people

A strong product engineer lands about one reviewed PR per working day. 87 harness PRs a day is roughly 80–90 engineers.

Add the triage, decomposition and review work behind 68 closed issues a day: another 10–15 senior reviewers and tech leads.

Understates it: harness PRs average ~920 added lines, larger than a typical human PR.

Method B · by volume

250–375 people

75.4K net lines a day, at a sustainable 200–300 net lines per engineer per day, is 250 to 375 engineers.

Overstates it: the count includes tests, plan and build-log files, and some generated code.

Honest midpoint

~155 engineers

plus a review and lead layer of about 15. Call it a 170-person product engineering org running every day. That is down from 310 last report, with the same ratio to Method A.

Caveats, stated plainly

  • The basis is list API rates, not an invoice. It answers "what would this output cost to buy", not "what was charged". The cash range is in the cost section.
  • The pricing formula got stricter. This report applies the fleet's own 1-hour cache-write rate and the grok 2× cliff, which the earlier reports skipped. On their method this window is $20.3K, not $21.5K.
  • The fleet slowed and this report doesn't say why. 39 PRs a day over the last four days, nothing from project-1 since Sep 25, nothing from project-5 since Oct 1, a 16-hour gap with no merges Oct 2–3. Spend fell in step, so this looks like no work queued, not a capacity limit. That was not checked.
  • Self-work is 72% of the window. 754 of 1,043 PRs went into cromford. They are real merged code, but they are the harness improving itself, not client product.
  • Two classification calls. cromford-builders[bot] (the Fly builders' new GitHub App) counts as harness. 64 PRs on lead/ branches under the operator's account count as operator work, the way Mac sessions always have. 27 merge-train PRs are excluded because their contents are already counted.
  • Coverage is still the open risk. A call that writes no cost row is invisible. OpenRouter's cash runs $1.9K ahead of its priced rows. 2,000 refusal and context-kill rows carry zero tokens. xAI, Fireworks and Codex have no spend endpoint.
  • The Anthropic meter is a weak check. It also bills Mac sessions, its history only goes back to Sep 23, it reset on Oct 1, and its latest snapshot came back with status "error".
  • Fly is now real money. $2,160 of machine time across 42 machines, 4.6× last window. It is excluded from the API-equivalent figure as infrastructure.
  • Lines are inflated. Each harness PR carries its plan and build-log files alongside code and tests. Method A (by PRs) is the safer headcount anchor; Method B is a ceiling.
  • Human rates are averages. $150K–$220K fully loaded per US engineer, $1,100 a day per lead. The calculator takes other numbers.
  • Not the same work. A 170-person org also ships design, product management, support and on-call. This compares engineering throughput only.
  • The potential row is an extrapolation. It combines the proven peak volume (Sep 18) with this window's cost per PR. No day has yet done both.
