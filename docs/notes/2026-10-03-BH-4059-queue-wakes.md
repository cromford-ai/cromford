# Queue wakes and the */10 fly backstops (BH-4172, last slice of BH-4059)

Doc: EVENTS, OPERATOR-SETUP

## What rings a `queue` wake today

One writer, from #4169: `POST /api/queue` in `worker/jobs/src/routes/queue.js`
(`queueWake`) writes a `queue` wake on repo `harness/build-queue` when a row
lands `queued` with `target=fly`. On an armed hub, `bin/lib/wake-class-queue.sh`
turns a drain's queue rows into one launch of `bin/fly-dispatch.sh` and one of
`bin/fly-pool-tick.sh`. The design, the dedupe and what that slice left out are
in `docs/notes/2026-10-03-BH-4169-queue-wake-tracer.md`; this note does not
restate them.

## What changed

The `fly-dispatch` and `fly-pool-tick` hub crons go from `*/2` to `*/10`
(`cron/lanes/190-fly-dispatch.cron`, `cron/lanes/240-fly-pool-tick.cron`), and
so do their registry cadences in `catalog/lanes.d/fly-dispatch.json`,
`fly-pool-tick.json` and `fly-pool.json` (which keeps its
`(via bin/fly-pool-tick.sh)` suffix). Both lines stay behind
`/root/harness/bin/hub-lease.sh check &&`. They are backstops now: they catch
whatever a wake missed, such as a `PATCH` re-queue, which writes no wake.

The cost: the class ships unarmed. Until it is armed, nothing launches these
two lanes early, so a fly build can wait up to 10 minutes (it was 2) to be
dispatched. Arm it on the hub, after the worker carrying #4169 is deployed:

    bin/jobs-client.sh flag-set wake-lanes-queue on "BH-4059"

## What did not change, and why

`bin/fly-results-ingest.sh sweep` stays at `*/2` (`cron/lanes/200-fly-results-ingest.cron`),
and no wake launches it.

- Its relay hold is sized in sweep intervals against the builder's 600 s idle
  autostop: `RELAY_HOLD_MAX_S` 240 plus up to two 120 s sweeps is 480, under
  600. See the header "THE ARITHMETIC IS THE WHOLE POINT" in
  `bin/fly-results-ingest.sh`. At `*/10` that bound breaks.
- Its sweep ingests a finished single-machine build and removes the hub's
  `<project>-active.json` before the builder autostops. If that file outlives
  the machine, `bin/fly-dispatch.sh` step (2) reads the stopped machine as
  "builder died mid-build" and requeues.
- It holds no flock, so a wake-launched copy racing the cron copy could ingest
  one RESULT twice (#4169's reason for leaving it off the wake path).

Test: `tests/fly-cron-backstop.bh-4172-test.sh` renders the hub crontab and the
registry and pins all three cadences.
